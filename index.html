The problem

Your people should not have to know which platform a conversation lives on.

Most organisations run two or three chat tools at once, plus an intranet nobody reads. The cost is not the licences — it is the daily tax of checking four places and losing the thread between them.

Parwe is being built to put all of it in one client. Native channels are first-class Matrix rooms. Conversations that still live in Slack or Teams arrive through official APIs and render as ordinary messages, with a small origin indicator and nothing else to learn. When a platform behind the wall changes, the chat experience does not.

Origin indicator

Bridged, not embedded.

A bridged message is a real message in your homeserver: searchable, quotable, kept under your retention policy. The only difference a person sees is one quiet label — and the fact that some formatting cannot survive the trip.

  • Parwe Relay talks to the official Slack and Microsoft Graph APIs.
  • No scraping, no unofficial clients, no shared credentials.
  • Turn a bridge off and the history stays behind in Parwe.

Agents

Agents are members, not integrations.

Parwe is being designed so that AI agents join the hub the way people do: as first-class members with their own identity, presence and permissions — invited to a channel, and removed, like anyone else.

Own identity

A real member with its own name, avatar and access. You always know who — or what — said it.

Full audit trail

Everything an agent does is an ordinary event in the timeline: visible, searchable, retained under the same policy. No invisible automation.

Human gates

Agents propose; people approve. A decision is one reaction in the channel, recorded like any other message.

Whole-hub context

Summarise a bridged Teams thread, watch a feed, draft a reply into Slack — it is all one inbox, so agents see all of it.

Your models. Agents run on your infrastructure with your keys — cloud models or local ones. Conversations never leave your servers to think. Different agents can run different models — and a channel’s policy can pin its agents to models that run in your own racks.

Self-hosting

Your conversations, your servers.

The reason to run a hub yourself is not cost. It is knowing where the messages are.

Closed federation

Your homeserver talks to your domains only. Nothing replicates to a public network.

Your identity provider

OIDC through Matrix Authentication Service. Leavers lose access when HR says so.

Retention you set

Per-room policies in your database, with exports that satisfy an auditor.

No licence fees

AGPLv3 and Apache-2.0. Read the code, fork it, run it for as many people as you like.
